Key Concepts

This page brings together the central ideas from the website so readers can quickly review the most important cybersecurity habits.

  • Strong passwords reduce the chance that attackers can guess or reuse your login details.
  • Multi-factor authentication adds another layer of protection even if a password is stolen.
  • Phishing awareness helps people avoid scams that use urgency or fear to encourage unsafe clicks.
  • Verification habits make users pause and confirm whether a message or link is trustworthy.
Concept Why it matters Best practice
Password strength Weak passwords are easier to guess and reuse. Use long, unique passphrases and a password manager.
Multi-factor authentication It blocks many attacks even when a password is compromised. Turn it on for email, banking, and school accounts.
Phishing awareness Scams often rely on emotional pressure rather than technical hacking. Check the sender, verify links, and slow down before clicking.
